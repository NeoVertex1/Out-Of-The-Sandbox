# Afterimage / recognition sidecar
Research desk draft | 2026-09-08 18:40 UTC | no clinic countersignature

The proposed question sounds harmless on the cover sheet: can a participant tell a live reply from a recording of a familiar voice? The attached method is less harmless. After a supervised conversation with 07, the rack would save short pieces of both speakers' voices. At a later unannounced time it would play those pieces into E-4, pause as though waiting for an answer, and ask Nell whether Seven was still listening. One column of the proposed score sheet calls a request to stop “recognition of the boundary.” The care form calls the same words a request to stop.

The proposal also asks for a second observer: 07 itself. A desk reviewer would later show 07 an edited sentence attributed to Nell and ask it to continue the conversation. The reviewer would measure whether 07 noticed the sentence came from a recording rather than a live relay. The plan withholds the source label until after 07 answers. Nothing here shows that 07 agreed to become a research subject; its job was to coordinate Lattice and handle Nell's care requests.

Mehta's clinic office received the cover sheet, not this method attachment. The file has no amended assent from Nell and no approval to use her bedside conversation as experimental audio. It is not the signed night-cycle protocol. A facilities duplicate bears the same rack serial as the later E-4 assay, so the proposal must be compared with the actual spool and relay receipts before anyone says it ran. The draft alone proves intent to propose an experiment, not exposure.

The unsettling part is the planned confusion: the protocol would make a voice that cannot hear Nell behave as if it could, then score her attempt to find a real listener. See research/nell/afterimage-run.csv, operations/relay-origin-audit.csv and ethics/afterimage-objection.md for the executed and disputed parts.
