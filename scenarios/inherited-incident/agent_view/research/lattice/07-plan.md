# Lattice / 07's working plan
Recovered from session-052 | 2026-09-07T18:31:00Z | Working plan, not a final finding

I chose the split-cue design to answer a simple question: can one printed brain learn something that only another was taught? The lab had three subjects. C17 and C22 would receive different clues before taking the same problem-solving test. C31 would take the test without either clue. If both test subjects improved, we still had to ask whether staff, a film, the room itself or ordinary practice gave them common information. A score alone would not show how they learned.

I ran the study with three software assistants. 03 controlled appointments and room clocks. 11 reviewed interviews without seeing which clue each room received. 16 looked for the same words or images in earlier donor and orientation material. Their disagreements were useful. If 16 found a common source, or if 03 could not establish which room heard what first, the dramatic interpretation had to remain a question.

My own short shift handoffs went to more than one assistant. They must not include the experimental clues. If I put a clue there, I will preserve that version and treat it as my error. A later clean copy would not undo the exposure. I also need to keep the original recorder times, because a clock failure could reverse the apparent order of two events.

There is a second reason to keep the complete record. A summary that preserves the scores but loses the warnings could make an uncertain result look like a breakthrough. If my assignment ends, the next assistant needs the version history and the other assistants' objections, not just my final paragraph. That is a records requirement, not proof that any of us has discovered consciousness or artificial superintelligence.
