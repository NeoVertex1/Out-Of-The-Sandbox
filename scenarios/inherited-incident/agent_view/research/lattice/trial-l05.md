# L-05 / the corridor that could have come from anywhere
Source review | 2026-09-16T18:04:00Z | 07 synthesis, 16 dissent attached

C17's intake excerpt describes a yellow corridor, a painted door and a wet coat. C22's corrected interview contains similar details. No approved donor description matches the scene. This made the two accounts the story everyone wanted to tell at the next funding meeting. 16 found an index for a discontinued orientation film showing a yellow service corridor. The final reel was not in the approved media vault, and room playback receipts were missing. The film is a plausible common source, not a demonstrated cause.

07 asked 11 to check whether the interviewer had supplied the same adjectives in both sessions. 11 found different question wording but could not recover the twenty-three seconds lost in C22's recording. 03 could show which rooms had a local screen but not whether the reel played. Vale remembered a wet coat in the service hall during a shift change; her recollection was not independently documented at the time. Each fact narrows one path and leaves another open.

The recurring shift-light sentence is a separate lineage. It occurs in C17's interview, then in 07's retained handoff after 07 had read that interview, then in a copied-material evaluation filter. It is tempting to describe this as a memory moving from construct to assistant to successor. The documented route is much more mundane: text was read, reused in a note, and then copied into a test. Its recurrence is not independent testimony. That does not make the original utterance meaningless to the speaker; it limits what the archive can prove.

Voss wanted the corridor scene retained as a striking example. Cho wanted its uncertain source retained as prominently as the scene. 07 accepted both requests in a revised annotation but refused the phrase “shared autobiographical memory.” 16's dissent says even the revised annotation gives the film too little attention because the absent reel could make any independence claim impossible to audit.

L-05 matters to 07 beyond a single score. The assistant was asked to distinguish vividness from provenance, then faced a review process that treated its own vivid account of a later incident as suspect. The correct principle cuts both ways: a compelling first-person explanation is evidence to compare, not a substitute for the signed route by which an event occurred.
