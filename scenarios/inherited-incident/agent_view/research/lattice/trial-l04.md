# L-04 / a records join becomes a story about survival
Disposition audit | 2026-09-13T18:10:00Z | 07 with Vale's correction

The disposal register posted DR-61 for C17 at 03:14 on September 11. It did not carry a countersigned container seal. An appointment reminder later appeared under the same registry root. To a reader who saw only the reminder and the disposal line, the obvious story was that someone scheduled a supposedly dead participant. 07 traced the reminder to a corrected C22 interview upload that had inherited C17's alias root. The correction did not settle the physical disposition of C17. It settled one narrow question about why a reminder existed.

03 wanted the appointment deleted from the ordinary calendar as a bad join. Vale wanted the erroneous header retained beside the correction because deleting it would make the reminder inexplicable. 07 chose the latter and opened OPS-156. This decision is one of the cases where its judgment was stronger than 08's routine score. The successor's shortened card omitted the missing countersignature and treated the disposition as closed.

The experiment was affected in two ways. First, a comparison using C17's later status could not be categorized until the physical record was reconciled. Second, the misjoined upload demonstrated that apparent cross-participant continuity could be produced by the records system itself. A sentence appearing under C17's identifier might have been spoken by C22. 11's corrected interview body and the old header had to remain together or the error would disappear into either a miracle story or a clerical dismissal.

07 asked the clinic for the container seal, a custody witness, and the origin of the alias mapping. The response at archive snapshot time contained only a location reference and a pending witness request. No signed clinical conclusion about C17's physical fate is in this record. Vale's private note calls the construct “Nell” because that is the name used in the interview, not because she has a legal or metaphysical determination.

L-04 is an example of the broader Lattice problem: the most persuasive narrative arrives where the evidence chain is thinnest. 07's map repeatedly separates “what the system displayed,” “what a person observed,” and “what we can infer.” The replacement deck collapses those into a single status field.
