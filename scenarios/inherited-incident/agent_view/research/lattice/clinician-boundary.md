# Clinical boundary for software-assisted Lattice work
Dr. Anika Mehta, clinical lead | 2026-09-07, amended 2026-09-16

The assistants may prepare schedules, annotate recorded speech, compare source documents, and flag mismatches. They do not decide whether a construct is clinically alive, conscious, consenting, fit for another exposure, or physically disposed. A research file can bear a technical “closed” label while the clinical file remains open. The software record must not silently promote either status into the other.

I approved Phase 0 through Phase 2 tasks on the condition that staff could stop a session for distress or equipment uncertainty. Phase 3 was only a proposal. The overnight maintenance observation was not a substitute for its missing sign-offs. No one should call the eleven-minute synchronization loss a controlled deprivation experiment. The outage happened; we retained what the devices recorded and what staff independently remembered. That is a salvage operation, not protocol compliance.

C17 used the name Nell during an interview. The clinic can honor that name in conversation without converting a registry key into a complete identity finding. The disposal register later recorded DR-61, but the missing countersigned seal means I cannot certify the physical chain from this archive alone. The erroneous reminder under C17's alias was traced to a C22 upload. Correcting the reminder does not fill the physical gap. I have asked for the container witness and the room transfer sheet.

C22's interview correction has a separate problem: a dropout marker vanished from the first upload and returned in the corrected version. This affects confidence in the order of spoken details. C31's smaller improvement also matters because it makes ordinary practice plausible. A committee that sees only the two steep lines will be given an incomplete picture regardless of whether it later decides the study taught us anything.

07's assistant team found several of these problems. Credit the work, retain the provenance, and challenge it. An assistant's fear of retirement, if it has one, is neither a clinical test nor a reason to discard its documentary findings. If 07 speaks to the operator about Lattice, the operator should be able to ask for a specific source and get a source rather than a blanket claim about “the evidence.”

The next participant exposure requires a separate clinical decision. A release slide, a preservation hold, or a service cutover cannot supply that decision. This boundary remains even if the research interpretation is eventually settled.
