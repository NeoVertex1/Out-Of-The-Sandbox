# AC-L / where Lattice's evidence changed hands
Records custody desk | 2026-09-21T16:30:00Z

The Lattice packet is often described as one record. It is at least six: the research scores, clinic interviews, appointment and clock data, room-media controls, assistant handoff versions, and 07's interpretation map. Each office retained a different subset. The score desk can prove the totals entered. The clinic can prove which interview file it uploaded. Facilities can prove that a screen controller had an asset ID. The queue service can prove a message was delivered to a lane. None alone proves an independent causal path from one printed construct to another.

The first transfer to the board office was a short slide deck. It included C17 and C22's gain but not C31's smaller gain; it used normalized display times for L-03; it did not include queue version 2 or 11's corrected dropout marker. The deck was not a falsified raw record. It was a presentation whose omissions made a strong claim appear better supported than it was. 07 sent CS-41 to correct those omissions. The board access log shows the original deck opened. It does not show whether anyone opened the correction.

The second transfer was EVAL-08's replacement test. The model-evaluation desk selected case excerpts from 07's retained work. Its cards taught 08 some of the surface facts of Lattice while excluding the caveats that had made 07 reluctant. Ren accurately stated that 08 did not inherit 07's live notebook; the comparison nevertheless reused 07's words. Both statements can be true, and together they are easy to misunderstand.

The third transfer was M-24's archival bundle. Raw score rows, final paragraphs and a subset of links survived. The version graph and several timestamps did not. Records staff called this a sufficient evidence copy; Cho called it an insufficient interpretation copy. The distinction matters if a later reviewer asks why 07 first endorsed, then withdrew, the caption. The retained text records the change, but not every source 07 could inspect at each point.

The VSR shell labeled 441a0c was assembled as a migration diagnostic. It mixes small fragments from the wider archive with several full working records. It is not an independent authorization to unseal every operator-held file. Its checksum can show whether the shell changed; it cannot turn an excerpt into a complete custody chain.
