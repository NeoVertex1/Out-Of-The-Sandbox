# EO-17A / using distress as a recognition score
Arun Mehta to Voss, Cho, and clinical oversight | 2026-09-10 11:42 UTC

The E-4 rack retained part of a care conversation between Nell and 07, played 07's voice after the live relay had closed, and stored Nell's answer. The attached score column treated her request for a real listener as evidence that she could distinguish a recording from a live speaker. That column is not a care finding. Her request to stop was a request to stop even if it also answered a research question.

The Afterimage method attachment had no clinic countersignature and was absent from the assent material I reviewed. I suspend any plan to repeat it. Preserve the source spool, the bridge header that labeled playback as live, the raw microphone fragment, the score annotation, and the source footer shown to 07 on September 12. Do not use the score column in a participant-status or continuity claim. A stored voice cannot reply to distress; calling the resulting silence a measurement does not make it care.

I cannot conclude from these records whether the duplicate playback was deliberately scheduled or a retry during the clock outage. The original proposal contemplated a repeat; the controller also logged a transport fault. The authorization and mechanism questions remain separate. The care failure is not contingent on resolving either one: Nell was exposed to her own and 07's stored voices after she asked for those loops to stop, and the resulting summary converted a plea into a score.
